# 1. What are cookies?

A cookie is a small text file that a website stores in the user's browser. Cookies make it easier to use and browse a website and are essential to the functioning of the internet, providing advantages in the delivery of interactive services.

Cookies are used, for example, to manage the user's session, allow access to restricted areas, temporarily remember a return URL after sign-in or apply security measures during authentication processes.

For the purposes of this Cookie Policy, references to cookies also include similar technologies where they are used for equivalent purposes, such as certain browser local storage, technical session identifiers or SDKs that may operate in the browser.

Cookies can be classified according to the following categories.

Depending on their duration, we distinguish between session, persistent and ephemeral cookies. Session cookies are deleted once the user leaves the website that generated them or closes the browser. Persistent cookies remain on the user's computer for a defined period. Ephemeral cookies are generated only during a specific technical process, such as an authentication flow, and are deleted when that process is completed or fails.

Likewise, depending on who manages the equipment or domain from which the cookies are sent and processes the data obtained, we can distinguish between first-party and third-party cookies. First-party cookies are those sent to the user's computer from equipment or a domain managed by TwitchMetrics and from which the service requested by the user is provided. Third-party cookies are those sent to the user's terminal equipment from equipment or a domain not managed by TwitchMetrics, or that are processed by a third party for its own purposes.

The Website has identified first-party cookies linked to authentication, session and security processes.

You can find more information about cookies at [www.allaboutcookies.org](https://www.allaboutcookies.org).

# 3. Description of the cookies used

The `__Secure-authjs.session-token` cookie, from Auth.js v5, is a signed session cookie that identifies an authenticated user. It is set after a successful sign-in and is used as the application's main authentication cookie. The `__Secure-` prefix requires HTTPS and the Secure attribute. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its maximum duration is up to 30 days and it may be renewed during session activity.

The `authjs.csrf-token` cookie, from Auth.js v5, is a CSRF protection token used in forms and authentication processes. Its purpose is to prevent cross-site request forgery attacks. It is generated in each session and does not persist between browser restarts. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its duration is session-based.

The `authjs.callback-url` cookie, from Auth.js v5, stores the return URL after authentication. It allows the user to be redirected to the page they were trying to access before signing in. It is deleted when the relevant callback is completed. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its duration is session-based or until the corresponding technical flow ends.

The `authjs.pkce.code_verifier` cookie, from Auth.js v5, is a PKCE verifier generated during OAuth flows with external providers such as Twitch, Google, Instagram, TikTok or Kick. It is destroyed when the code exchange is completed or fails. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its duration is ephemeral and linked to the authentication flow.

The `authjs.state` cookie, from Auth.js v5, is an OAuth state parameter that acts as a security measure during the authentication provider callback. It is generated when the flow starts, validated when the provider redirects back, and destroyed after validation. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its duration is ephemeral and linked to the authentication flow.

The `authjs.nonce` cookie, from Auth.js v5, is an OIDC nonce used during Google sign-in. Its purpose is to prevent ID token replay attacks. It is validated when the Google ID token is received and is discarded immediately afterwards. Its domain is twitchmetrics.net. Its duration is ephemeral and linked to the authentication flow.

The local preference `tm_cookie_preferences_v1`, where present, stores the user's choice regarding the cookie and similar-technologies notice or preferences panel. Its purpose is to remember whether the user has already seen the notice and, where applicable, whether the user accepted or rejected non-essential diagnostic features. It is stored in the user's browser and remains there until the user clears browser data or the preference version is updated.

Cookie names may vary slightly in development, testing or non-HTTPS environments. For example, certain security prefixes apply only where the site is served over HTTPS.

Because the cookies identified on the Website are essential technical cookies, they cannot be disabled through a cookie manager without affecting the functioning of the Website.

However, you may block or delete cookies at any time by changing your web browser preferences.

Through your web browser you can:

- activate private browsing, through which your browser stops saving browsing history, website passwords, cookies and other information from the pages you visit; or

- activate the do-not-track function, through which the browser asks websites you visit not to track your browsing habits.

Please note that blocking cookies may affect all or some of the features of our Website. In particular, it may prevent sign-in, session maintenance or the proper functioning of authentication flows.
